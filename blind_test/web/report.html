Abstract

Modern social media and algorithmic feeds have led to an unprecedented failure of text. As human communication is mediated through automated curation, short-video clips, and algorithmic feeds, original subjective intentions are systematically distorted. In this project, we treat generative ambient music as an analog for digital media itself. We built A Machine for Misunderstanding, an experimental human-computer-human installation forcing two participants in isolated rooms to converse solely through generative synthesis driven by language models.

Across 10 sessions and 31 complete interaction cycles, our double-blind data proves that the acoustic medium introduces substantially greater distortion than the language model itself ($d_{\text{medium}} > d_{\text{model}}$ in 7 of 10 sessions), and that positive affective intentions are routinely inverted into alarming acoustic experiences. This experiment renders visceral how computational media fundamentally destabilizes human expression.

1. Introduction: Media and the Breakdown of Language

1.1 The Medium is the Message in the Algorithmic Age

Marshall McLuhan famously formulated that "the medium is the message": the infrastructure through which we communicate is never a neutral container; it actively reshapes, filters, and defines the content it carries. In contemporary social media, human expression is passed through opaque recommender networks and viral compression. Words lose their visceral context. We believe we are speaking to another human, but we are actually speaking into a computational medium that reformulates our intentions, creating widespread social alienation, affective polarization, and chronic misunderstanding.

1.2 Music as an Extreme Form of Mediation

Instead of designing an AI tool to "optimize" or "clarify" communication, we built a system to expose this rupture. A sender inputs their real-time emotional and bodily state in natural text. An AI model interprets these words into 4-dimensional psychological coordinates $\{V, A, D, C\}$, which directly modulate an analog-style modular synthesizer (VCV Rack). A blind receiver hears the resulting continuous generative soundscape and attempts to reconstruct what the sender originally felt. By treating ambient sound as media infrastructure, the experiment dramatizes how mediation inevitably shears human connection.

3. Data Processing & Metric Formulation

To mathematically compute communicative distortion, emotional states are projected onto the classical three-dimensional Valence-Arousal-Dominance (VAD) circumplex space (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974; Russell, 1980):

Valence ($V \in [0, 1]$)
Pleasantness: Dysphoria vs. Euphoria
Arousal ($A \in [0, 1]$)
Activation: Soporific calm vs. Kinetic frenzy
Dominance ($D \in [0, 1]$)
Control: Overwhelmed vs. Autonomous power

All coordinates are normalized vectors in $[0, 1]^3$: $$\vec{x}_{\text{sender}} = \begin{bmatrix} V_s \\ A_s \\ D_s \end{bmatrix}, \quad \vec{x}_{\text{model}} = \begin{bmatrix} V_m \\ A_m \\ D_m \end{bmatrix}, \quad \vec{x}_{\text{receiver}} = \begin{bmatrix} V_r \\ A_r \\ D_r \end{bmatrix}$$

The normalized Euclidean divergence $d(\vec{x}_a, \vec{x}_b) \in [0, 1]$ is scaled by the diagonal of the unit cube ($\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$): $$d(\vec{x}_a, \vec{x}_b) = \frac{\|\vec{x}_a - \vec{x}_b\|_2}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \sqrt{(V_a - V_b)^2 + (A_a - A_b)^2 + (D_a - D_b)^2}$$

Model Gap ($d_{\text{model}}$)
$d(\vec{x}_{\text{sender}}, \vec{x}_{\text{model}})$
Error between human writing and LLM semantic decoding.
Medium Gap ($d_{\text{medium}}$)
$d(\vec{x}_{\text{model}}, \vec{x}_{\text{receiver}})$
Error introduced purely by the synthesizer's acoustic medium.
Total Misunderstanding ($d_{\text{total}}$)
$d(\vec{x}_{\text{sender}}, \vec{x}_{\text{receiver}})$
Net divergence between sender emotion and receiver perception.

Key Analytical Discoveries

  1. The Acoustic Medium Dominates Distortion ($d_{\text{medium}} > d_{\text{model}}$ in 7 of 10 sessions): Natural language processing captures sentiment with modest deviation ($\mu = 0.1980$), but physical transductions across synthetic timbre introduce severe entropy ($\mu = 0.2984$), skewing perception by +50.7%.
  2. Frontier Models Cannot Bypass Sensory Ambiguity: DeepSeek (deepseek-chat, Session 4) slashed text inference error by 26.6% and latency by 80.5% relative to the local model in Session 3, yet receiver misunderstanding persisted ($d_{\text{medium}} = 0.2161$). Scaling computational intelligence does not eliminate the physical impedance of the medium.

7. Conclusion

The primary empirical finding of A Machine for Misunderstanding is that when human expression is forced through an automated intermediary, the medium itself introduces substantially more variance than the words themselves ($d_{\text{medium}} > d_{\text{model}}$ in 7 of 10 sessions). Natural language models are increasingly capable of approximating semantic intent; however, once those semantic representations are re-embodied into physical, continuous media—whether analog synthesizer voltages or algorithmic social media feeds—the structural properties of the medium dominate the interpretation. The medium does not merely transmit meaning; it possesses its own acoustic and affective agency that reshapes and alienates original intentions.

In contemporary digital culture, where authentic interpersonal connection is increasingly replaced by algorithmic feeds and flattened digital text, understanding this communicative gap is urgent. By quantifying how meaning shears and drifts across generative mediation, this study demonstrates that seamless digital communication is an illusion. Recognizing the inevitable distortion introduced by media is the first step toward reclaiming mindful, critical engagement with the computational systems that structure modern human interaction.
